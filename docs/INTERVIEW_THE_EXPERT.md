# Interview the expert: 20 minutes that a manual cannot give you

Ask someone who has done the work: an operator, an inspector, a crew lead, a district
engineer. Take notes in their words. Everything you collect goes into your skill's
`references/practice.md` (or `references/glossary.md`), with their role and the date, and becomes a suggestion or a hard case, never
a rule on its own.

## Before you start

- Say what the notes are for, and that their name will not be written down (their role will).
- Bring the manual and two or three real or synthetic cases.

## Questions

**Words**
1. What do you and the crews call <the manual's term>? What do you call <another>?
2. Which words in the manual does nobody actually use?

**Order and timing**
3. Walk me through the first three hours after <the event>. What do you do first, and why?
4. Which steps in the manual happen in a different order in practice?

**Gaps and decisions**
5. The manual says <a person> decides <X>. How do they decide? What do they look at?
6. What does the manual not cover that comes up every time?

**Mistakes**
7. What do new staff get wrong in their first <storm, inspection, season>?
8. Show me a report, card or form you would send back. What is wrong with it?

**Exceptions and safety**
9. When do you not follow the manual word for word? What would make you overrule it?
10. What should a computer never decide here?

## After the interview

- Write each idea as one entry: what they do, why, when it applies, who decides.
- Mark anything that contradicts the manual. Keep both.
- Read the entries back to the expert (or send them) and fix what they correct.
- Re-run your test scenarios with and without the new entries. Report what changed.
