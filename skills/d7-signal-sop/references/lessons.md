# Lessons from past storms and other documents (NOT the D7 SOP)

These lessons come from documents written after real storms. None of them is a step in the
D7 SOP v1.5. Use them only as **suggestions**, in the "Suggestion" format of
`assets/action_card.md`:

- cite the lesson by its id (L1, L2, ...), never as an SOP clause;
- always set **Person decides: yes** and name who;
- never present a lesson as required, and never let it override an SOP step.

Each lesson is paraphrased from its source. Open the source before quoting it.

## L1
- **Lesson:** If a generator cannot power a signal, put stop control in place at major intersections.
- **Source:** FDOT Central Office, Traffic Engineering and Operations Office, "FDOT Hurricane Preparation and Response Recommendations" (slides; undated), "After the Hurricane".
- **Use when:** a signal is red (non-operational) and no generator is available or one cannot be connected.
- **Who decides:** TMC personnel, with the maintaining agency.

## L2
- **Lesson:** After an emergency, retime signals, starting with detour routes and heavily traveled areas.
- **Source:** FDOT Central Office, Traffic Engineering and Operations Office, "FDOT Hurricane Preparation and Response Recommendations" (slides; undated), "After the Hurricane".
- **Use when:** a road or bridge closure sends traffic onto a detour, or signals come back on commercial power.
- **Who decides:** the signal timing engineer for the corridor.

## L3
- **Lesson:** Removing signal heads before a storm is contested. For: it saves equipment, lightens span-wire loads and gets intersections back to normal sooner. Against: fewer indications than the MUTCD requires leaves the agency exposed; a three-day forecast can change track, so the removal may be unnecessary; removed heads can be mistaken for storm damage and replaced.
- **Source:** FDOT Central Office, Traffic Engineering and Operations Office, "FDOT Hurricane Preparation and Response Recommendations" (slides; undated), "Area of Controversy: Signal Head Removal". The slides list these as considerations, not recommendations.
- **Use when:** someone asks whether to take heads down before landfall. Give both sides; do not recommend either.
- **Who decides:** not stated in the D7 SOP; ask district traffic operations.

## L4
- **Lesson:** In Hillsborough County, more than 300 county-owned intersections have solar-powered backup beacons, developed by county Public Works after Hurricane Irma. They flash red, turning the intersection into a four-way stop, only after the signal loses power and its battery backup (about six to eight hours) is spent. About 170 signals were affected after Hurricane Ian in 2022.
- **Source:** Hillsborough County news release, originally published November 9, 2022 (reposted January 2, 2024), "A one-of-a-kind innovation makes Hillsborough County roads safer during widespread and extended power outages".
- **Use when:** ranking dark or flashing signals in Hillsborough County. A county intersection may be on battery or beacon, so the time since power loss matters.
- **Who decides:** TMC personnel, after checking with Hillsborough County.

## L5
- **Lesson:** In Pinellas County, numerous span-wire signals fell during Hurricane Debby (2024), leaving them inoperable and blocking roads; Hurricane Milton downed over 100 traffic signals in St. Petersburg. The county is replacing span wire with mast arms and elevating signal cabinets on evacuation routes, which are particularly vulnerable to flooding on barrier-island roads.
- **Source:** St. Pete Catalyst, "County seeks funding to storm-harden traffic signals", May 13, 2025, reporting Pinellas County documents.
- **Use when:** a span-wire signal is down, or a cabinet on a barrier-island or evacuation route may have flooded.
- **Who decides:** the field crew lead and TMC personnel. A downed signal is a road hazard first.

## L6
- **Lesson (why eSTORM matters):** After Hurricane Michael (2018), FDOT District 3 concluded it needed efficient, reliable, real-time tools for signal status and damage. With eSTORM after Hurricane Sally (2020), crews assessed 450 signals in the app, found 69 damaged, flashing or dark, and had all signals operational within one week.
- **Source:** National Operations Center of Excellence (NOCoE), case study "eSTORM", December 28, 2023, sponsored by FDOT.
- **Use when:** explaining why the SOP's eSTORM steps come first. It is a reason, not an extra step.
- **Who decides:** not a decision.
